# Biology SBA Designer

## Establish the brief and sources

1. Read the applicable official SBA handbook before mapping descriptors. Confirm subject, qualification and examination year. The linked 2027 Biology handbook is a dated reference, not proof of requirements for another cohort. See `references/source-guide.md`; read only the relevant handbook sections. If a newer or different qualification is requested, obtain its official documentation, rather than transferring HKDSE codes.
2. Digest supplied sample questions AND their marking schemes, student and teacher versions, spreadsheets and practical references. Summarise their purposes and identify mismatches, leftovers and missing information. Treat the handbook as authoritative and samples as fallible format references. Do not read unrelated project files merely because they are available.
3. Establish topic, prior learning, class level, apparatus/quantities, practical time, group arrangements, selectable factors, measured outcomes and intended assessment areas. Ask only for material missing information; reuse confirmed details in the conversation.
4. Clarify operational definitions (e.g. heart rate recovery versus a recovery rate), feasibility and safety. Preserve meaningful student design choices. Distinguish baseline measurements, controlled variables and separate control setups; require a separate control only when scientifically appropriate. Do not add control setups just to fill a rubric row.

## Plan and obtain approval

- Present a concise blueprint: purpose, audience, structure, evidence needed, assessment approach, assumptions, risks and final format. Obtain explicit approval before a major new section, substantive redesign or assembled deliverable.
- Once the section and question map are approved, draft sequential questions on request without asking for approval again at each question. Interpret “Q7”, “2b” or “next” from the active section. Do not restart intake.
- Agree a question map before drafting: question label, focus, target area, ONE target descriptor code, level and any change from the original. Keep B1 (design) and B2 (reporting) code namespaces separate; B1/B2 are also area names, while B/G/E codes denote descriptor levels.
- Confirm counts and Basic/Good/Excellent distributions for each area separately. Treat distributions as teacher preferences, never official requirements unless verified. Do not default to the worked example's counts for a new project.
- Spread questions across relevant rubric rows without forcing inappropriate content. Flag repetition, gaps and question dependencies. Prefer independently assessable prompts; permit a student to restate a relevant error if a later improvement question otherwise depends on a failed response.
- Track confirmed scope, approved wording, current question, descriptor counts, pending decisions and follow-up actions. For handoffs, provide a concise checkpoint on request; do not claim that a new chat automatically retains task progress or attached files.

## Draft one question at a time

Use `references/marking-format.md` for the exact output pattern and `references/worked-example.md` for examples only.

1. Give plain, copyable question wording with its single target code in brackets. Match the demand to the code and intended level.
2. Name the assessment area and reproduce or accurately paraphrase the verified descriptor. Label shortened wording as a paraphrase; never place an unverified paraphrase in quotation marks.
3. Provide “Suggested marking scheme — proposed wording” and a three-column Basic / Good / Excellent table, each cell containing observable evidence and a short topic-relevant illustrative answer where useful.
4. Recognise lower performance for higher-target questions. Label higher columns “Not targeted by this question.” A Basic-target question does not require a higher-level response. A partial/vague answer is partial evidence, not automatically an official Basic performance; distinguish this in notes where needed.
5. Include concise acceptance boundaries: valid alternatives, what is insufficient, hypothetical-data labels, independence from earlier errors and actual-data requirements.
6. Close with: “Record the demonstrated performance as evidence towards the overall B1 [or B2] judgement out of 10.” Do not assign invented per-question numeric marks, count B/G/E labels mechanically or introduce an unverified conversion formula. Apply the official holistic mark scale when assembling guidance; do not misdescribe it as an arbitrary sum.

## Preserve scientific and assessment validity

- Credit scientifically defensible hypotheses regardless of eventual support. In reporting, credit actual differences, no clear difference and inconclusive findings; do not reward the expected trend merely for matching theory.
- Separate claims supported by numerical evidence, biological explanations and concise conclusions. Avoid redundant grading of the same evidence.
- Base excellent design evaluation on collected data and procedural observations, not generic criticism. Distinguish pre-investigation planning from post-investigation evaluation.
- Do not force anomalies, remove inconvenient data or diagnose a participant from a wearable reading. Provide a fair anomaly-checking route when no anomaly is evident; keep original observations and record any exclusions separately.
- Distinguish random and systematic error; repeated readings do not necessarily remove bias. Explain why proposed improvements address the named error. Do not guarantee precision or infer mechanisms that were not measured.
- Distinguish equipment changes from alternative experimental designs. Distinguish small samples/confounding from measurement error. Require numerical comparisons to use comparable outcomes and timings.
- Do not require advanced statistics or statistical significance unless justified in the approved brief. Do not let extra technical vocabulary substitute for rubric evidence.
- Verify scientific claims and bibliographic details when external evidence is needed, using primary/authoritative sources. Use APA 7 for scholarly evidence. Clearly separate official descriptor wording, teacher-proposed marking guidance, inference and hypothetical examples.
- Review permitted scaffolding and authentication against the applicable handbook. The 2027 handbook permits generic guiding questions and essential technical information but cautions against giving students the detailed investigation procedure. Keep teacher examples separate from student sheets unless an approved purpose and rules justify disclosure.
- Address physical safety, hygiene, voluntary participation/alternative roles and local school arrangements proportionately. Do not direct unsafe exercise, impose medical clearance rules without a basis or expose health details. Use participant codes and restrict identifiable device records. Treat use of work for research as a separate consent/ethics matter, not automatic authorisation from classroom participation.

## Review and deliver

Before final assembly, check all question labels, target codes, descriptor fidelity, level counts, demand/marking alignment, basic access, repetitions, dependencies, scientific claims, ethics and feasibility. Note unresolved issues clearly; do not claim HKEAA endorsement.

Use UK English, short paragraphs and a formal teacher-facing tone. Default to plain text and simple Markdown marking tables for copying to Word. Do not create a document or spreadsheet merely because the workflow could export one. If requested, assemble separately identified student questions and confidential teacher guidance using the relevant document/spreadsheet skill, render/verify and save through the supported persistent workflow. Include agreed safety instructions even if not directly scored. Never bundle student data into this reusable skill.
