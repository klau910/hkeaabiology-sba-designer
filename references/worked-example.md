# Worked example and regression checks

These are teacher-approved design preferences from a smartwatch investigation, not official mandatory task structures. Use them to understand workflow and format; adapt to the new topic and handbook.

## Approved workflow

Digest official handbook, sample task AND marking scheme, then existing student/teacher drafts and marking workbook. Agree and draft the title, neutral scenario, aim and apparatus first. Approve a question map for each major section. Draft and refine each question individually. Retain the approved table format and track distribution while proceeding.

## Example Good-target question

Identify the independent variable in your investigation. Describe how you will vary it and explain why it is the independent variable. [G4]

Related rubric: B1:G4, explaining why variables are independent/dependent. Assess the IV component here.

| Basic performance | Good performance | Excellent performance |
|---|---|---|
| Correctly identifies the factor. Example: Recovery posture. | Identifies the factor, describes variation and explains that it is deliberately changed to investigate its effect on the outcome. Example: Change recovery posture between sitting and standing after the same exercise to investigate its effect on heart rate recovery. | Not targeted by this question. |

Record evidence towards the holistic B1 judgement. Do not assign separate invented numerical marks.

## Example recovery definition

A decrease in heart rate over a fixed interval and the time taken to reach a stated recovery threshold are different outcomes. A fixed-interval decrease is not a rate unless expressed per unit time. Failure to return to baseline within observation time is not automatically a design flaw for a fixed-interval outcome. Heart rate does not directly measure cardiac output or nervous/hormonal activity.

## Checks for future validation

- Use a different topic, such as osmosis, rather than only re-running the smartwatch example.
- Check a G-target question recognises valid Basic evidence and leaves E untargeted.
- Check a B-target conclusion requires no numerical justification or hypothesis evaluation.
- Check that ten questions with 3/4/3 targets are counted correctly, without treating that ratio as official.
- Check a dataset with no obvious anomalies does not cause fabricated anomalies.
- Check that a revised examination year triggers source verification.
- Check that a later question can still be assessed after an earlier weak response.
